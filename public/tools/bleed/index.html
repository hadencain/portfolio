bleed

bleed channel-surfs live broadcast (or any video you feed it), datamoshes the picture until it melts, then reads that decoded melt back as sound. The video corruption is the instrument — colour and signal bleed across frames become noise-music stems you drop straight into a DAW.

how it works
01

capture & mosh

Segments are pulled live from broadcast streams (or split from a local file), then datamoshed — the MPEG-4 keyframes are stripped so the decoder hallucinates a continuous smear as one clip bleeds into the next.

02

sonify the melt

The melt only exists at decode time, so the decoded frames are read as sound through two lanes: a scanline wavetable that scans pixel rows, and a spectral resynthesizer that treats each frame as a spectrum. Motion in the picture bends pitch and scatters the sound — a bloom you see is a burst you hear.

03

tame & render

Each lane runs a curated chain — chop, wash, grit, with a safety limiter always on — and writes three 48 kHz stems (scan, spectral, mix) plus the moshed video. Deterministic per seed; the examples below are rendered from a synthetic generative source.

running it

bleed is a local tool — it needs ffmpeg and a network connection for live capture, so it runs on your machine rather than in the browser. It ships with a queue-based web UI (python -m iptvmosh.web) for firing off several renders at once and browsing a saved library, and a CLI (python -m iptvmosh noise) for one-offs and scripting.

Every render is deterministic per seed, and any output can be fed back in as the next source — each pass compounds the melt further.
